  # Content Creation Prompt #1: Contrastive Analysis (French-English Comparisons)

**Project**: OffGrid English - Offline-First English Learning for Cameroon
**Created for**: ESL Educators / Content Creators
**Date**: October 28, 2025

---

## Purpose

Help Francophone learners understand **WHY** they make specific errors by showing side-by-side French vs. English grammar structures.

When students make mistakes, they can tap a "💡 French Comparison" button to see how French grammar interferes with English, making the learning process transparent and effective.

---

## Target Quantity

**40-50 contrastive analysis entries** covering the 7 grammar categories:

| Grammar Category | Number of Entries |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Tense & Form | 10-12 entries |
| Subject-Verb Agreement | 8-10 entries |
| Prepositions | 8-10 entries |
| Word Order | 6-8 entries |
| Plurality | 4-6 entries |
| Articles | 4-6 entries |
| Auxiliaries | 4-6 entries |
| **TOTAL** | **40-50 entries** |

---

## What You Need to Provide

For each of the **40-50 selected items** (high-frequency error questions), please provide:

1. **French Structure** - How this concept works in French (with example)
2. **English Structure** - How it works in English (with example)
3. **Why This Is Hard** - Explain the interference pattern in 1-2 sentences
4. **Visual Cue** (optional) - Suggest which part to highlight in red (French interference) and green (correct English)

---

## Template

```
Item ID: [e.g., tense-15-A]
Original Question: "Yesterday, I ___ to the market." (Options: go, went, goes)
Correct Answer: went

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Hier, je suis allé au marché"
(French uses "être" (to be) + past participle for movement verbs)

English Structure:
"Yesterday, I went to the market"
(English uses simple past tense: go → went)

Why This Is Hard:
French speakers often think in compound past (passé composé: "I am gone") and may say "I am went" or forget to change "go" to "went" because French past participles don't always change the verb form as obviously.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED (French interference): "I am went" or "I go"
- GREEN (Correct English): "I went"
```

## Examples by Grammar Category

### Example 1: Subject-Verb Agreement

```
Item ID: sv-8-A
Original Question: "The teacher ___ English every day."
Options: teach, teaches, teaching
Correct Answer: teaches

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Le professeur enseigne l'anglais chaque jour"
(French: "professeur" can be masculine or feminine, but verb ending is same)

English Structure:
"The teacher teaches English every day"
(English: 3rd person singular requires -s/-es ending)

Why This Is Hard:
In French, many verb endings sound the same even when written differently
(je parle, tu parles, il/elle parle - all pronounced "parl"). French speakers
may not hear or notice the -s ending in English because it's not as prominent
in French.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "The teacher teach"
- GREEN: "The teacher teaches"
```

---

### Example 2: Prepositions

```
Item ID: prep-12-A
Original Question: "I wait ___ my friend at the bus stop."
Options: for, to, at
Correct Answer: for

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"J'attends mon ami à l'arrêt de bus"
(French: attendre = to wait, followed directly by object, no preposition needed)

English Structure:
"I wait FOR my friend at the bus stop"
(English: "wait" requires "for" before the object)

Why This Is Hard:
French speakers may say "I wait my friend" because "attendre" doesn't need a
preposition in French. The direct translation creates an error.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "I wait my friend" (missing FOR)
- GREEN: "I wait FOR my friend"
```

---

### Example 3: Tense & Form

```
Item ID: tense-23-A
Original Question: "She ___ to school every day."
Options: go, goes, going
Correct Answer: goes

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Elle va à l'école tous les jours"
(French: "va" is 3rd person singular of "aller", but the change from infinitive
is very different: aller → va)

English Structure:
"She goes to school every day"
(English: "go" adds -es for 3rd person singular: go → goes)

Why This Is Hard:
French verbs change completely (aller → va), so French speakers might not
notice that English only adds -es. They may use the base form "go" because
the change seems too small to matter.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "She go to school"
- GREEN: "She goes to school"
```

---

### Example 4: Articles

```
Item ID: articles-5-A
Original Question: "___ life is beautiful."
Options: The, A, [no article]
Correct Answer: [no article]

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"LA vie est belle"
(French: abstract nouns take definite article "la/le")

English Structure:
"Life is beautiful"
(English: abstract nouns used generally don't need articles)

Why This Is Hard:
French speakers directly translate "la vie" as "THE life" because French
requires the article. In English, general abstract concepts (life, love,
happiness) don't use articles.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "THE life is beautiful"
- GREEN: "Life is beautiful"
```

---

### Example 5: Word Order

```
Item ID: word-9-A
Original Question: "I always eat breakfast at 7 AM."
Which word order is correct?
Options:
A) I eat always breakfast at 7 AM
B) I always eat breakfast at 7 AM
C) Always I eat breakfast at 7 AM
Correct Answer: B

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Je mange toujours le petit-déjeuner à 7 heures"
(French: Adverb "toujours" can come after verb: "mange toujours")

English Structure:
"I always eat breakfast at 7 AM"
(English: Adverb "always" comes before main verb)

Why This Is Hard:
French speakers might say "I eat always breakfast" because in French the
adverb often comes after the verb. English has stricter adverb placement
rules: before main verb, after "be" verb.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "I eat always breakfast"
- GREEN: "I always eat breakfast"
```

---

### Example 6: Plurality

```
Item ID: plurality-6-A
Original Question: "There are many ___ in the classroom."
Options: student, students, student's
Correct Answer: students

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Il y a beaucoup d'étudiants dans la salle de classe"
(French: plural is often shown by article "les" or "des", not always by
changing the noun. Some plurals are silent: étudiant vs étudiants sounds same)

English Structure:
"There are many students in the classroom"
(English: plural always adds -s/-es, and you must write AND pronounce it)

Why This Is Hard:
Many French plurals are silent (étudiant/étudiants pronounced the same), so
French speakers may forget to add -s in English because they're not used to
hearing/pronouncing plural endings.

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "many student"
- GREEN: "many students"
```

---

### Example 7: Auxiliaries

```
Item ID: aux-7-A
Original Question: "___ you speak English?"
Options: Do, Does, Are
Correct Answer: Do

CONTRASTIVE ANALYSIS:

French Structure:
"Parlez-vous anglais?" or "Est-ce que vous parlez anglais?"
(French: Invert verb-subject OR use "est-ce que" structure)

English Structure:
"Do you speak English?"
(English: Use auxiliary "do/does" + base verb for questions)

Why This Is Hard:
French doesn't use an auxiliary verb like "do" for simple questions. French
speakers might say "Speak you English?" (direct inversion like French) or
"You speak English?" (statement with rising intonation).

Visual Highlighting:
- RED: "Speak you English?" or "You speak English?"
- GREEN: "Do you speak English?"
```

## Priority Items to Annotate

Please prioritize items that students get wrong most frequently. Focus on:

✅ Items with **<60% first-attempt accuracy** (if you have this data)
✅ Common error patterns from research (Sokeng, 2014):
   - Tense/form (23% of errors)
   - Subject-verb agreement (22% of errors)
   - Prepositions (18% of errors)
✅ Items where the **French structure directly interferes** with English

## Quality Checklist

For each contrastive analysis entry, ensure:

- [ ] French and English structures are shown with **actual example sentences**
- [ ] Explanation is **clear and concise** (1-2 sentences)
- [ ] Focuses on the **interference pattern** (why French speakers make this mistake)
- [ ] Uses **simple language** (remember, learners are reading this)
- [ ] Includes **visual highlighting suggestions** (red for error, green for correct)
- [ ] Examples use **realistic, everyday contexts**
